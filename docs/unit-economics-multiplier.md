# Unit economics and server-to-cash multiplier

The model treats infrastructure as a control plane rather than a hosting resale. A Hetzner EX44 server is a scenario unit. The discussion explored £1 of server spend producing approximately £750–£900 net FCF to Kanay through shared P1/PCO twins, outcome-linked spreads and wholesale block aggregation.

The model is only valid after subtracting hosting, utilisation, support, acquisition, payment, tax, bad debt, verification, compliance and partner costs. Sensitivity cases should vary occupancy, price, retention, outcome capture and settlement delay.

Illustrative companion assumptions included P1 revenue of £720/entity/year, PCO revenue of £6,150/entity/year and planning revenue of £800–£1,200 per £1 spend. These are not forecasts. A 10–20% outcome spread means Kanay captures a defined share of verified improvement or procurement spread after the customer receives value; the contract must specify baseline, measurement, cap, dispute process and settlement timing.

The flywheel is liquid FCF → high-ROCE physical assets → lower internal LCOE → wider wholesale-to-retail spread → more FCF. Unit economics are therefore a capital-allocation hypothesis requiring live validation.
