# Chronological developmental script

This document records the programme from first principles to prototype. The initial problem was economic fragmentation: households and companies hold balance sheets, flows, risks, health, energy and operational data in disconnected systems, causing effort to be spent on survival rather than strategic capital allocation.

## 1. P1 and PCO
P1 is a persistent virtual person or household twin. PCO is its enterprise analogue: a virtual company, site or operating unit. Both maintain state, constraints, commitments, assets, risks, missions, evidence and realised cash. Their legal contexts differ, but their control grammar is isomorphic: baseline, floor, risk, intervention, verification, settlement and reinvestment.

## 2. From passive survival to allocation
A warning dashboard was rejected. Pulse must show the survival floor and free cash flow, but Dispatch must turn telemetry into missions, aggregate demand, settle transactions and allocate capital toward productive assets.

## 3. Gross-up breakthrough
For £3,600 net utilities at a simplified 40% marginal rate, G × 0.60 = £3,600, so G = £6,000. The often-cited £6,207 figure implies additional assumptions: £6,207 × 0.60 = £3,724.20, and the effective burden required to leave exactly £3,600 is about 42.0%. It therefore cannot be derived from the simple 40% equation alone. ITEPA 2003 s204 makes benefit valuation and employment context relevant; corporate BIK structures may reduce friction but require benefit classification, PAYE/NIC and professional UK tax review.

## 4. Occam onboarding
The onboarding debate rejected full data import first. The sequence became statement drop → unhedged risk report → zero-risk activation switch → evidence and expansion. The user sees the cost of inaction before being asked for broad permissions.

## 5. Two surfaces
Sovereign Pulse answers what is true now: balance sheet, survival floor, FCF ticker and telemetry. Dispatch answers what changes next: autonomous missions, wholesale block aggregation, approvals, settlement and evidence. The closed loop is Pulse → Dispatch → settlement → verified outcome → Pulse.

## 6. North stars and taxonomy
P1 uses WELLBY, a wellbeing/life-years outcome. PCO uses CCE = FCF / gross operating profit, with a planning target above 80%. The seven value tiers are energy; shelter/plant; health and vitality; climate/externalities; nutrition/COGS; mobility/logistics; capital/identity.

## 7. Multiplier and prototype
The server-to-cash model explored £1 of Hetzner EX44 infrastructure generating £750–£900 net FCF through outcome spreads and wholesale aggregation. This is a hypothesis requiring sensitivity testing. Screens evolved from Statement and Risk through Activation, Pulse, Dispatch, Risk/controls and P1→PCO Spawner. The result is a visual operating system rather than a passive dashboard.
